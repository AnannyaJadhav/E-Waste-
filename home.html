About Us

A Welcome to Smart Collectors, a sustainable initiative built to make e-waste collection simple, smart, and rewarding.

At Smart Collectors, we believe that small actions can create a big impact. Every year, tons of electronic waste end up in landfills, harming our environment and wasting valuable materials. Our mission is to change that — by connecting societies, residential areas, and offices directly with certified e-waste recyclers through an easy-to-use platform.

We organize e-waste collection drives, pick up discarded electronics responsibly, and ensure they reach formal recycling sectors for safe disposal and reuse. In return, we share a part of the profit with participating communities — because we believe sustainability should benefit everyone.

With Smart Collectors, you’re not just getting rid of waste — you’re joining a movement towards a greener, cleaner, and smarter future.

What Is E-Waste?

Common items in e-waste streams include computers, mobile phones, large household appliances, and medical equipment. Millions of tonnes of e-waste are recycled using unsound activities, as well as being stored in homes and warehouses, dumped, and illegally exported. When e-waste is recycling using unsound activities, it can release up to 1000 different chemical substances into the environment, including known neurotoxicants such as lead (3). Pregnant women and children are particularly vulnerable due to their pathways of exposure and developmental status. The International Labour Organization (ILO) estimates that 16.5 million children were working in the industrial sector in 2020, of which waste processing is a subsector.

Key facts:

  • Global e-waste: 50+ million tons/year
  • Only 20% is formally recycled
  • Contains toxic materials like lead and mercury

How to Dispose E-Waste

Step 1: Back Up & Erase Data

Before disposing of any device, back up important files and securely erase all personal data.

Step 2: Check Local Recycling Centers

Find certified e-waste recycling centers near you using our map or local government resources.

Step 3: Donate Working Devices

If your device still works, consider donating it to schools, NGOs, or community programs.

Step 4: Use Certified Recyclers

Ensure your e-waste is handled by certified recyclers who follow safe and eco-friendly practices.
